## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Meridian segments, evaluates, and manages its customer base, and gives AI analysts a structured way to investigate customer-level business questions that go beyond simple SQL aggregation — such as "why is a high-revenue customer barely profitable?"

## 2. Customer Segmentation Framework

Meridian classifies every customer into one of three Segments, recorded on every order:

| Segment | Profile | Strategic Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Consumer** | Individual buyers, highest order count | Volume growth, retention via loyalty/promotions |
| **Corporate** | Registered business accounts | Account growth, negotiated contract value |
| **Home Office** | Small business / remote workers | Cross-sell Technology + Office Supplies, frequency growth |

Consumer represents Meridian's largest Segment by total Sales and order count, with Corporate second and Home Office third. Despite this ranking, all three Segments realize broadly similar aggregate Profit Margins, indicating that Segment size does not by itself predict Segment profitability — margin differences are driven more by *what* and *how* a Segment buys (Category mix, discount behavior) than by *how much* it buys.

## 3. Customer Value and Purchasing Behavior

Meridian evaluates customer value along three dimensions, which must be assessed **together**, never individually:

1. **Revenue contribution (Sales)** — how much a customer buys.
2. **Profitability (Profit, Profit Margin)** — how much Meridian actually earns from that customer.
3. **Engagement (order count, purchase frequency)** — how consistently the customer transacts.

A customer who ranks highly on Sales does not automatically rank highly on Profit — Meridian's own transaction history contains customers with substantial cumulative Sales who are only marginally profitable, or in some cases net **unprofitable**, once discount and shipping cost are netted out. This is the central reason Meridian never uses Sales rank alone as a proxy for "best customer."

## 4. High-Value vs. High-Discount Customers

Meridian distinguishes between two customer archetypes that are easy to conflate:

- **High-value customer**: strong Sales **and** healthy Profit Margin, indicating a genuinely profitable relationship.
- **High-volume, low-margin customer**: strong Sales but thin or negative Profit, typically associated with heavy discount usage, a Category mix skewed toward low-margin Sub-Categories (e.g., Furniture/Tables), or high Shipping Cost relative to order value.

Both archetypes can appear near the top of a simple "Top Customers by Sales" report, which is exactly why Sales-only customer rankings are insufficient for account strategy — Profit and Profit Margin must be reviewed alongside Sales for every customer under review.

## 5. Customer Concentration Risk

Meridian monitors what share of total company Sales is generated by its largest customers. In Meridian's transaction history, even the **top 20 customers by Sales together represent only a low single-digit percentage of total company Sales**, out of a customer base of roughly 4,800 distinct customers. This indicates a **low customer concentration risk** at the company level — Meridian's revenue is broadly distributed across a large customer base rather than dependent on a small number of key accounts.

This has two implications for analysts:

- Company-wide performance swings are very unlikely to be explained by the behavior of any single customer; look instead at Segment, Category, or Region-level shifts.
- Concentration risk should still be assessed **within** a Region, Market, or Category, where a handful of large accounts may represent a much larger share of that narrower base even though they are small relative to the whole company.

## 6. Regional Customer Behavior

Customer purchasing patterns vary meaningfully by Region and Market, consistent with the differences already documented in `pricing_discount_policy.md` and `shipping_fulfillment_policy.md`:

- Markets and Regions with higher average Discount tend to also show thinner Profit Margins at the customer level, independent of Segment.
- Regions with a Furniture-heavy purchase mix will show structurally lower customer-level Profit Margins than Regions skewed toward Technology, purely due to Category economics (see `product_category_management.md`) — this is a **composition effect**, not necessarily a sign of poor account management.

## 7. Discount Behavior by Customer Segment

- **Corporate** and **Home Office** accounts are more likely to carry negotiated or recurring discount arrangements, which should be evaluated at the account-relationship level (cumulative Profit across all their orders) rather than penalizing any single discounted order in isolation.
- **Consumer** discounting tends to track promotional campaigns and seasonality more than individual negotiation.
- A rising average Discount within a Segment, without a corresponding increase in order count or Quantity, is a discount-discipline warning sign consistent with the escalation criteria in `pricing_discount_policy.md`.

## 8. Investigating "Sales Are Increasing but Profit Is Decreasing" (Customer-Level)

When this pattern appears for an individual customer or account, the recommended investigation sequence is:

1. **Confirm the pattern** — compute the customer's Sales and Profit trend over the same comparison periods (see `root_cause_analysis_playbook.md`, Steps 1–3).
2. **Check Discount trend** — has average Discount on this customer's orders increased?
3. **Check Category/Sub-Category mix** — has the customer shifted toward lower-margin Sub-Categories (e.g., more Furniture, less Technology)?
4. **Check Shipping Cost** — has this customer's orders shifted toward faster Ship Modes or higher-cost destinations?
5. **Classify findings as FACT / INFERENCE / UNKNOWN** per `business_analysis_governance.md` — e.g., "Discount rose from 10% to 28% on this account's orders" is a FACT; "the account manager over-discounted to hit a quota" is an UNKNOWN unless corroborated outside the transaction data.

## 9. Investigating "One Customer Generates Significant Revenue but Very Little Profit"

This is a documented pattern in Meridian's own transaction history — several customers with large cumulative Sales realize thin or even negative cumulative Profit. Recommended investigation sequence:

1. Pull the customer's full order history: Sales, Profit, Discount, Category mix, Shipping Cost, Ship Mode.
2. Identify whether the low profitability is concentrated in a few orders (a handful of deeply discounted or high-shipping-cost transactions) or spread evenly across the relationship.
3. Check whether the customer's Category mix skews toward structurally low-margin Sub-Categories.
4. If the account is Corporate/Home Office, check whether a negotiated discount rate is simply not sustainable given the customer's Category mix — this may require a pricing/contract review rather than a "root cause fix."
5. Present findings as FACT (what the data shows), INFERENCE (a plausible business explanation), and UNKNOWN (anything requiring account-manager or contract input).

## 10. Investigating "One Customer Segment Suddenly Declines"

1. Confirm the decline is real and material (Steps 1–3 of the root-cause playbook) — Segment-level Sales/Profit/order-count trend over the relevant period.
2. Decompose by Category, Region, and Market within that Segment to see if the decline is broad-based or concentrated.
3. Check whether Discount behavior changed for that Segment in the same period.
4. Check whether order count declined (fewer customers/orders) versus average order value declined (same customers, smaller baskets) — these imply very different business problems (retention vs. basket size).
5. Explicitly state what the transaction data **cannot** explain (e.g., a competitor promotion targeting that Segment, a policy change affecting Corporate procurement) as UNKNOWN limitations.

## 11. Recommended Customer KPIs

- Customer-level Sales, Profit, and Profit Margin (aggregate, not row-averaged)
- Customer order count and average order value
- Top-N customer concentration (% of total Sales from top 10/20/50 customers)
- Segment-level Profit Margin trend
- Segment-level average Discount trend
- Customer count per Segment, trended over time (a proxy for acquisition/retention health)

## 12. Escalation Rules

Escalate a customer or Segment finding when:

- A customer's cumulative Profit is negative despite meaningful cumulative Sales.
- A Segment's aggregate Profit Margin declines materially while Sales are flat or growing (a "profitless growth" pattern).
- Concentration within a Region/Market rises sharply (a small number of accounts now represent a large share of that geography's Sales).
- A Segment's average Discount rises materially without a corresponding rise in order volume.

### Document Scope

**This document can establish:** Meridian's fictional customer segmentation framework, the correct multi-dimensional way to evaluate customer/Segment value, and a structured investigation methodology for common customer-level anomalies, informed by real aggregate patterns in the transaction dataset (e.g., low overall customer concentration; presence of high-Sales/low-Profit customers).

**This document cannot establish:** the specific real-world reason any individual customer's behavior changed, actual customer satisfaction or churn (not recorded in the dataset), or account-management decisions made outside the transaction system. These require SQL-verified customer-level analysis plus, where necessary, human account-team input.
